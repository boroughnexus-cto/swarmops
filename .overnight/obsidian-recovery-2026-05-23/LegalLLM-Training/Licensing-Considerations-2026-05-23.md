## Part 2 — Comparator models on disk

### `Equall/SaulLM-141B-Instruct` — MIT, with upstream Mixtral Apache 2.0

Equall's card states `License: MIT-License`. The base is continuous pretraining of Mixtral 8x22B (Apache 2.0). Treat the combined work as **Apache 2.0 + MIT**: preserve upstream Mixtral NOTICE, include both copyright notices, cite the SaulLM paper (arXiv:2407.19584).

### `meta-llama/Llama-4-Scout-17B-16E-Instruct` — Llama 4 Community License (April 5, 2025)

Commercial use permitted **with conditions:**
- **700M MAU threshold:** must request a separate licence from Meta if >700M MAU. Not relevant at Briefbox scale.
- **Attribution:** display "Built with Llama"; fine-tune naming must start "Llama-".
- **IP termination:** any IP litigation against Meta auto-terminates the licence.
- **AUP — unauthorized practice of law clause:** *"the unauthorized or unlicensed practice of any profession including, but not limited to, financial, legal, medical/health, or related professional practices."* Standard industry reading: enabling licensed professionals is permitted; providing legal advice directly to consumers without solicitor oversight is not. **Document clearly that Briefbox augments qualified solicitors rather than dispensing advice to the public.**

### `MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-M2` / `MiniMax-M2.7` — "Modified MIT" — ❌ COMMERCIAL USE PROHIBITED

Despite the `license: mit` tag on HF, the actual LICENSE in the repo adds:

> *"Any Commercial Use of the Software or any derivative work thereof is prohibited without obtaining a separate, prior written authorization from MiniMax. To request such authorization, please contact api@minimax.io with the subject line 'M2 licensing'."*

The definition of Commercial Use explicitly includes *"the deployment or provision of the Software or its derivatives that have been subjected to post-training, fine-tuning, instruction-tuning, or any other form of modification, for any commercial purpose."*

**Recommendation:** Drop MiniMax from the production stack, or email api@minimax.io requesting commercial authorisation.

## Part 4 — Frontier-API Terms (the seed-bank training-data risk)

### OpenAI — Services Agreement (effective Dec 1, 2025)

**§Restrictions, clause (e):** *"except for a Permitted Exception, use Output to develop artificial intelligence models that compete with OpenAI's products and services."*

**Permitted Exception:** *"'Permitted Exception' means Customer using Output to: (a) develop artificial intelligence models primarily intended to categorize, classify, or organize data (e.g., embeddings or classifiers), if these models are not distributed or made commercially available to third parties; and (b) fine tune or customize models provided as part of OpenAI's fine-tuning or other Services."*

The carve-out covers (a) embeddings/classifiers and (b) fine-tuning OpenAI's own models — **not third-party generative LLMs**.

### Anthropic — Commercial Terms (effective June 17, 2025)

**§D.4 (Use Restrictions):** *"Customer may not and must not attempt to (a) access the Services to build a competing product or service, including to train competing AI models or resell the Services except as expressly approved by Anthropic"*

### Google — Gemini API ToS (effective March 23, 2026) + Cloud Service Terms (Feb 18, 2026)

**Gemini API:** *"You may not use the Services to develop models that compete with the Services (e.g., Gemini API or Google AI Studio)."*

**Google Cloud:** *"Customer will not use an AI/ML Service or Generated Output to develop a similar or competing product or service... Customer will not use output from an AI/ML Service (including Generated Output) to substitute, replace, or circumvent the use of a Google Model, directly or indirectly, or create or improve models similar to a Google Model."*

**Of the three frontier providers, Google's terms are the broadest.**

### GitHub Copilot Business / Enterprise — Product Specific Terms (March 5, 2026)

The Copilot product terms themselves do **not** add an explicit "no training derivative models" clause. However, upstream model-provider restrictions almost certainly **flow through** — accessing GPT-5.5 / Claude Sonnet / Gemini via Copilot does **not** launder the underlying training restrictions.

## Part 5 — What this means in practice

**Industry interpretation of the "compete" clauses:**
- **Purposive reading (vendor-favourable):** A legal-domain assistant for solicitors is plausibly "competing" with Claude/Gemini/GPT.
- **Narrow reading (defender-favourable):** "Compete" means head-to-head substitution at the API/model layer, not at the application layer. **Not been tested in court.**
- **OpenAI's Permitted Exception** is the only explicit safe harbour and it does **not** cover generative LLM fine-tuning on third-party bases.

### Reference dispute

**OpenAI–DeepSeek distillation allegations** (Jan 2025) — only public, high-profile example. OpenAI accused DeepSeek of distilling outputs; OpenAI did **not** sue. No final ToS ruling on AI-output distillation has been reached in US or UK courts as of mid-2026.

### Mitigation paths

1. **Use only the gpt-oss-120b seed-bank pathway.** Outputs from self-hosted gpt-oss-120b (Apache 2.0) carry no provider restrictions.
2. **Negotiate a commercial agreement** with each provider.
3. **Quarantine the existing 73,500 seeds.**
4. **Lean on open-licence sources** — SaulLM (MIT), Mixtral (Apache), gpt-oss-120b.
